# I. INTRODUCTION

## A. Operational Background and Edge Deployment Realities
Pervasive encryption has fundamentally reshaped perimeter network defense. Transport-layer protocols such as TLS 1.3, QUIC, and encrypted tunneling standards (e.g., WireGuard, IPsec) now carry the vast majority of enterprise and consumer traffic [1]. These protocols strip network middleboxes of plaintext payload visibility [2]. Traditional Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) engines—historically reliant on regular expression matching against cleartext application strings—are largely obsolete [2]. Because datagram payloads appear as high-entropy, pseudo-random bitstreams, modern Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) and perimeter monitoring engines must infer application intent, service identity, and malicious tunneling exclusively from observable transmission dynamics. Threat detectors must extract signatures from directional packet lengths and inter-arrival times (IAT) observed during the opening handshake exchanges of each session [3].

Deep representation learning has largely displaced manual traffic feature engineering in this domain. Researchers have explored diverse model families to process raw packet timing and length distributions: multimodal deep learning architectures (MIMETIC) [4], bidirectional recurrent sequence models (FS-Net) [5], semi-supervised graph fingerprinting frameworks (FlowPrint) [6], pre-trained datagram transformers (ET-BERT) [7], and multi-phase attribute models (MPAF) [8]. These neural classifiers map multi-channel packet dynamics into latent feature spaces capable of separating encrypted services at line rate.

Deploying automated classifiers into Security Operations Center (SOC) pipelines requires transparent, verifiable decision logic. When network alerts feed Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR) playbooks, high-confidence detections trigger automated actions: populating security information and event management (SIEM) dashboards, prioritizing analyst triage queues, isolating compromised endpoints, and synthesizing dynamic Access Control List (ACL) drop rules in packet filtering engines (e.g., `iptables` or `nftables`). Security operators must verify whether an alert stems from authentic protocol semantics—such as TLS Client Hello cipher suite negotiations—or from spurious background artifacts such as transient TCP window sizes or ephemeral local port numbers. Consequently, security researchers have adopted post-hoc Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks—such as TreeSHAP, KernelSHAP, and LIME—to audit intrusion detection systems and traffic classifiers, as demonstrated by Sarhan et al. [9], Mali et al. [10], and Sharma [11].

Recent findings in computer vision [12] and natural language processing [13] indicate that model compression can destabilize internal representations. Yet within compact, one-dimensional network traffic classifiers, the stability of decision logic under integer quantization remains unstudied.

At the same time, real-time threat detection forces inference out of centralized cloud environments and onto physical perimeter hardware. Residential gateways, enterprise switches, and Customer Premises Equipment (CPE) must inspect traffic at line rate without introducing queuing jitter. Edge appliances operate under strict physical limits. Low-power ARM and MIPS cores possess small on-chip memory hierarchies where Level-1 data (L1-D) caches rarely exceed 32 to 64 KB per core. Storing full 32-bit floating-point (FP32) weight matrices and allocating intermediate activation tensors causes cache thrashing, high DRAM access latency, and memory bus saturation. Fixed-point integer quantization mitigates these hardware bottlenecks by mapping continuous 32-bit floating-point parameters to discrete 8-bit integers (INT8) [14]. Under optimized runtimes such as PyTorch QNNPACK, INT8 execution utilizes 128-bit ARM NEON vector registers to compute sixteen 8-bit integer multiply-accumulate operations in parallel, shrinking model storage by up to 75% and accelerating inference.

Quantization typically follows two primary workflows [14]:
1. *Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):* A fast, data-efficient calibration step. An unlabelled validation set determines discrete affine scale ($S$) and zero-point ($Z$) parameters that project pre-trained floating-point weights and activation tensors onto integer grids without structural parameter retraining.
2. *Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):* A retraining scheme that models rounding and clipping truncation during the forward pass using simulated fake-quantization operators. The non-differentiable step functions are bypassed in the backward pass using the Straight-Through Estimator (STE), allowing stochastic gradient descent (SGD) to guide weights into parameter basins resilient to low-precision truncation.

## B. Predictive Invariance versus Explanatory Fidelity
To audit black-box classifiers, the cybersecurity community relies on post-hoc attribution methods such as KernelSHAP [15]. These methods compute local attribution vectors $\boldsymbol{\phi} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ assigning importance values to each input feature token (e.g., the directional byte count or inter-arrival interval of an observed packet). Analysts inspect these attributions to confirm that models track genuine protocol negotiation patterns rather than learning transient transport-layer noise.

Edge engineering, however, evaluates quantized models almost exclusively on predictive accuracy invariance [14]. If an INT8 model matches the top-line accuracy, Macro $F_1$, or Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) of its FP32 parent within one percentage point ($\Delta \text{Acc} < 1\%$), practitioners declare the compression lossless. Implicit in this evaluation is the assumption that preserved predictive outputs guarantee preserved decision logic.

Integer quantization breaks this assumption. Deep neural networks are overparameterized, non-convex systems with substantial internal redundancy. In complex temporal sequence spaces, numerous distinct weight configurations yield identical classification predictions on a given evaluation benchmark while relying on completely different input feature subsets. As Hooker et al. demonstrated in overparameterized networks, model compression can selectively discard specific subpopulation representations without visibly degrading top-line accuracy metrics [16]. When applied to sequential network telemetry, compression risks reshaping decision surfaces without warning.

This logical divergence creates concrete operational hazards across security environments:
1. *Silent Logic Corruption:* A quantized perimeter firewall model may classify an obfuscated VPN tunnel with 98% recall, yet base its inference on background packet padding or TCP window artifacts rather than the handshake signatures recognized by the FP32 baseline.
2. *Brittle Defense Synthesis:* If an upstream SOAR system inspects an FP32 reference model to generate dynamic ACL filtering rules, but the deployed edge switch runs an INT8 model operating on restructured feature dependencies, the resulting firewall rules will target packet attributes the edge appliance ignores. Attackers can easily bypass these mismatched defenses.
3. *Regulatory and Compliance Exposure:* International governance mandates increasingly require auditable model explanations. Article 22 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) restricts automated decision-making that produces legal or significant operational impacts without meaningful human-interpretable logic [17]. Similarly, the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689) designates AI systems deployed as safety components in critical digital infrastructure (Annex III) as *high-risk*, demanding technical transparency (Article 13) and effective human oversight (Article 14) [18]. In the United States, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) requires models to demonstrate reliability and explainability throughout their deployment lifecycles [19]. If an organization audits an uncompressed development model while running a quantized engine with divergent attribution pathways, the resulting transparency documentation is legally invalid.

Understanding how feature attributions drift under fixed-point quantization is an operational requirement for edge network defense.

## C. Problem Statement and Formal Research Questions
This paper provides a forensic investigation into feature attribution stability, output calibration, and hardware execution dynamics in low-bit quantized deep traffic classifiers deployed on edge runtimes. We define **Attribution Drift** as the geometric rotation, magnitude redistribution, and rank reorganization of local input feature attributions across the model compression and edge serialization process.

We structure our investigation around four Research Questions (RQs):
- **RQ1 (Attribution Fidelity vs. Explainer Noise Floor):** *Does static Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) induce feature attribution drift that exceeds the intrinsic Monte Carlo sampling stochasticity of the explainer itself, and does the integer model preserve dominant protocol feature rankings?*
- **RQ2 (Structural Restructuring in Retrained Models):** *How does Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)—which retrains weights under simulated integer truncation—alter the geometric orientation, magnitude concentration, and monotonic rank ordering of feature attributions relative to the uncompressed baseline?*
- **RQ3 (Causal Disentanglement of Quantization vs. Optimization):** *Is attribution drift in retrained models caused by discrete integer rounding noise, or is it an artifact of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) trajectory displacement across an overparameterized loss surface?*
- **RQ4 (Numerical Serialization, Saturation Ceilings, and Execution Dynamics):** *How do fixed-point integer operator serialization and runtime execution constraints affect output probability calibration, operational decision boundaries, and inference latency across streaming versus batched edge deployment regimes?*

## D. Summary of Empirical Discoveries
To answer these questions, we develop **XDrift**, an end-to-end benchmarking framework evaluated on the Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC) **ISCX VPN-NonVPN 2016** dataset [1]. Prior traffic classification studies frequently suffer from cross-split session leakage, yielding overly optimistic evaluation metrics. To eliminate this flaw, our ingestion engine reconstructs **339,009 bidirectional network flows** from 140 raw PCAP/PCAPNG capture files ($24.42\text{ GiB}$) and enforces a strict, leakage-free **canonical session-group partition** across 106 independent sessions. This creates a held-out test set of **$N = 49,251$ bidirectional flows** from 16 canonical sessions, maintaining the natural class imbalance of operational networks ($2,693$ VPN flows representing a $5.47\%$ minority class vs. $46,558$ Non-VPN flows).

We train a lightweight edge 1D-CNN (`Traffic1DCNN`, 44,129 parameters) that ingests $(2, 64)$ feature tensors (Channel 0: directional packet length normalized to $[-1, +1]$; Channel 1: log-scaled inter-arrival time in $[0, 1]$), optimized with positive class loss weighting ($\text{pos\_weight} = 30.3756$). We compile this model to INT8 via static PTQ (HistogramObserver calibration on 69,564 validation flows) and QAT (5-epoch fine-tuning via STE) under the PyTorch QNNPACK ARM64 backend. To evaluate attribution stability, we apply **Masked KernelSHAP** ($M=50$ background reference flows, $K=150$ coalition evaluations per flow) across a stratified test cohort of $N = 100$ flows ($50$ VPN and $50$ Non-VPN flows, sequence lengths 4 to 64 packets), strictly masking zero-padded positions to avoid synthetic ranking inflation.

Our experimental investigation yields six primary empirical discoveries:
1. *The Explainer Noise Floor:* Perturbation-based explainers exhibit non-trivial Monte Carlo sampling stochasticity. By running KernelSHAP across three independent random seeds (Seeds 42, 43, 44) on the identical, uncompressed FP32 model, we establish the empirical noise floor: Mean Top-5 Intersection-over-Union ($\text{IoU}_5$) $= 0.9553 \pm 0.1829$, Signed Cosine Similarity ($S_c$) $= 0.9676 \pm 0.1450$, Total Variation Distance ($\text{TV}$) $= 0.0553 \pm 0.1588$, and Spearman rank correlation ($r_s$) $= 0.9516 \pm 0.1666$. Any attribution shift failing to exceed these boundaries represents explainer variance rather than true model drift.
2. *PTQ Preserves Dominant Decision Logic:* Contrary to fears of catastrophic explainability collapse, static PTQ preserves primary feature attributions with high fidelity. In **95 of the 100 cohort flows**, PTQ selects the exact same Top-5 features as the uncompressed FP32 baseline ($\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9786 \pm 0.0970$), maintaining directional alignment ($S_c = 0.9918 \pm 0.0394$) and minimal mass displacement ($\text{TV} = 0.0369 \pm 0.0620$). A non-parametric paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test against the noise floor detects a small positive rank shift ($W = 1506.0, R^+ = 3544.0 > R^- = 1506.0, p = 0.000459$), but the median displacement is negligible ($\text{median } \Delta \text{TV} = +0.004908$). For static quantization, explanations generated from floating-point reference models transfer reliably to edge appliances.
3. *QAT Induces Severe Structural Restructuring:* Although QAT yields strong test classification performance ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9834$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.6478$, calibrated $F_1 = 0.7762$), its internal attribution geometry collapses. Signed cosine similarity falls to $S_c = 0.4830 \pm 0.3449$ ($p < 10^{-17}$), Total Variation surges more than six-fold to $\text{TV} = 0.2346 \pm 0.1908$, and only $28\%$ of flows retain identical Top-5 features. On minority VPN flows, directional alignment drops to $S_c = 0.1665 \pm 0.1406$ (median $0.1341$, Mann-Whitney $U = 89.0, p = 1.24 \times 10^{-15}$). QAT navigates to an alternative parameter space that achieves high predictive accuracy through restructured decision logic.
4. *Causal Disentanglement via the FP32-FT Control:* To isolate the root cause of attribution drift, we implement an unquantized fine-tuning control baseline (`FP32-FT`) trained with the identical AdamW optimizer, learning rate, and loss schedule as QAT, but in full 32-bit floating-point precision. FP32-FT causes massive attribution reorganization ($\text{TV} = 0.2214 \pm 0.2327$, median $0.2070$; $S_c = 0.1166 \pm 0.8106$; $\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9481$). A paired Wilcoxon equivalence test confirms that attribution drift between QAT and FP32-FT is statistically indistinguishable ($W = 2390.0, p = 0.684, \text{median } \Delta \text{TV} = -0.0047$). This proves that **attribution restructuring is driven by gradient descent optimization traversing an overparameterized loss landscape**, not by integer quantization noise.
5. *Output Layer Saturation and the Threshold Collapse Pitfall:* Under QNNPACK integer serialization, the output linear layer's unsigned 8-bit quantization parameters ($S = 0.02535125, Z = 215, q_{\max} = 255$) impose a mathematical ceiling on the pre-sigmoid logit ($z_{\max} = 1.01405$). This ceiling caps predicted probabilities at $p_{\max} = \sigma(1.01405) = \mathbf{0.733812}$, clamping **$98.11\%$ of all test VPN flows ($2,642$ of $2,693$)** to the identical maximal score. Directly transferring the baseline operating threshold ($\tau = 0.900$) to the deployed QAT model causes **total operational blackout: zero positive calls, $0.0\%$ recall, and an $F_1$-score of $0.0000$**. Threshold recalibration on validation data ($\tau = 0.680$) fully restores detection capability ($F_1 = 0.7762$, recall $= 98.33\%$).
6. *Microarchitectural Edge Latency Duality:* Profiling on Apple Silicon ARM64 reveals a distinct operational division. Under single-flow streaming inspection ($B = 1$), INT8 quantization achieves only a $1.05\times$ speedup ($0.267\text{ ms} \to 0.255\text{ ms}$) because runtime interpretation, memory allocation, and kernel dispatch consume $>99\%$ of CPU cycles. In contrast, under micro-batched triage ($B = 64$), SIMD vectorization across 128-bit ARM NEON registers delivers an order-of-magnitude **$12\times$ throughput acceleration** ($6,387 \to 76,265\text{ flows/s}$, scaling up to $82\text{k flows/s}$). Additionally, compressing the checkpoint by $3.56\times$ ($184.2\text{ KB} \to 51.8\text{ KB}$) fits the static model entirely within on-chip L1 data caches.

## E. Concrete Research Contributions
This paper provides four concrete contributions to edge intelligence, network security, and explainable machine learning:
1. *Methodological & Forensic Benchmarking Protocol:* We formulate an empirical protocol for evaluating XAI stability under model compression. This includes: (i) an uncompressed multi-seed explainer noise floor protocol to separate model drift from Monte Carlo sampling jitter; (ii) temporal sequence masking to eliminate synthetic ranking inflation over zero-padded tokens; and (iii) a leakage-free canonical session-grouping pipeline (106 groups, 339,009 flows) that guarantees zero cross-split session contamination on the ISCX VPN-NonVPN benchmark.
2. *Empirical Disentanglement of Quantization vs. Optimization:* We introduce and evaluate an unquantized fine-tuning control baseline (`FP32-FT`). Non-parametric paired Wilcoxon equivalence testing ($W = 2390.0, p = 0.684$) proves that QAT attribution restructuring is caused by gradient descent optimization settling into an alternative parameter basin across an overparameterized empirical loss surface, whereas discrete integer quantization alone (PTQ) preserves attribution geometry ($\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9786, S_c = 0.9918$).
3. *Mechanistic Derivation of Output Saturation & Threshold Dynamics:* We derive and empirically confirm the discrete UINT8 serialization ceiling ($z_{\max} = 1.01405 \implies p_{\max} = 0.733812$) that clamps $98.11\%$ of minority VPN flows. We demonstrate that naive threshold transfer causes complete detection collapse ($F_1 = 0.0000$), establish post-quantization validation recalibration as a necessary deployment requirement ($F_1 = 0.7762$), and explain why aggregate Brier and ECE metrics mask confidence distortion under majority-class probability compression.
4. *Microarchitectural Profiling of Edge Inference Engines:* We present a microarchitectural characterization of fixed-point network traffic classification on ARM64 hardware running PyTorch QNNPACK. We measure the boundary between framework-bound streaming execution ($1.05\times$ latency reduction) and compute-bound batched SIMD acceleration ($11.94\times$ to $12.77\times$ throughput scaling, $76\text{k}$--$82\text{k flows/s}$), and demonstrate that the $3.56\times$ footprint reduction (51.8 KB) enables persistent L1-D cache residency on edge processors.

## F. Paper Organization Roadmap
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: 
- **Section II (Related Work):** Reviews the evolution of encrypted traffic representation learning, fixed-point neural network quantization architectures, post-hoc explainability methods in network security, and establishes a comparative taxonomy positioning XDrift against existing literature.
- **Section III (Methodology & Forensic Protocols):** Details the canonical session-grouping pipeline, the 1D-CNN architecture, the mathematical formulation of PTQ and QAT quantization operators under PyTorch QNNPACK, the implementation of the unquantized FP32-FT confounder control, the Masked KernelSHAP attribution engine, and the paired non-parametric statistical framework.
- **Section IV (Experimental Results & Hardware Benchmarks):** Evaluates physical ARM64 hardware latency and throughput, predictive classification metrics across the 49,251-flow held-out test partition, mathematical derivation and empirical verification of output probability saturation, attribution drift across the 100-flow cohort, causal disentanglement testing, and 16-group clustered bootstrap inference.
- **Section V (Discussion & Operational Security Implications):** Examines the operational security consequences of attribution drift in SOAR pipelines, analyzes compliance implications under the EU AI Act and GDPR, evaluates the limitations of empirical session skew, and outlines defensive engineering guidelines for edge AI deployment.
- **Section VI (Conclusion):** Concludes the paper and outlines future directions for attribution-regularized edge quantization objectives.
