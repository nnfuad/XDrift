# I. INTRODUCTION

## A. Operational Background and Network Edge Constraints
Modern enterprise and perimeter network security architectures are undergoing a profound operational transformation driven by two converging technological shifts: the universal adoption of end-to-end transport-layer encryption and the decentralization of automated threat inspection to resource-constrained edge appliances. The near-ubiquitous deployment of protocols such as Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3), QUIC, DNS over HTTPS/TLS (DoH/DoT), and Virtual Private Network (VPN) encapsulation has permanently rendered traditional Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) obsolete [1], [2]. Because packet payloads are rendered cryptographically indistinguishable from high-entropy random bitstreams, modern Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) and traffic monitoring engines must infer intent, application identity, and tunneling anomalies exclusively from observable packet dynamics—specifically, directional packet lengths and inter-arrival times (IAT) observed during the nascent phases of communication [3]–[5].

To extract actionable security intelligence from these subtle temporal signatures without manual feature engineering, deep representation learning has emerged as the preeminent paradigm [6]–[8]. Compact one-dimensional Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs) and temporal sequence models are increasingly deployed to ingest multi-channel datagram representations (e.g., directional packet lengths and log-transformed arrival intervals across initial flow segments) to detect obfuscated tunneling, malware command-and-control (C2) channels, and protocol abuse in real time [7], [9]. Concurrently, modern network defense paradigms mandate that threat inspection occur directly at the perimeter—on physical residential gateways, enterprise top-of-rack switches, Customer Premises Equipment (CPE), and edge security appliances—to enforce line-rate microsegmentation and isolate malicious flows before they traverse enterprise demilitarized zones (DMZs) [16].

Deploying deep neural classifiers on perimeter hardware, however, encounters severe microarchitectural bottlenecks. Edge networking appliances are governed by strictly bounded power envelopes, modest compute capabilities (typically low-power ARM or MIPS cores), and severe memory hierarchies where on-chip Level-1 data (L1-D) caches rarely exceed 32 to 64 KB per core [16]. Storing full 32-bit floating-point (FP32) network weights and buffering large intermediate activation tensors incurs prohibitive cache thrashing, high DRAM access latency, and excessive memory bus contention. To mitigate these constraints, fixed-point integer quantization—specifically mapping 32-bit floating-point parameters to discrete 8-bit signed/unsigned integers (INT8)—has become the de facto industrial standard for edge compilation [17]–[20]. Through low-level inference runtimes such as PyTorch QNNPACK [27] and ARM NEON vector extensions, INT8 execution promises up to a $4\times$ reduction in static memory footprint and substantial throughput acceleration by evaluating multiple integer multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations within single 128-bit Single Instruction, Multiple Data (SIMD) vector registers.

Quantization is conventionally deployed via two primary paradigms:
1. **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):** A computationally lightweight, data-efficient procedure wherein the dynamic ranges of pre-trained floating-point weights and intermediate activation tensors are calibrated on a small set of unlabelled validation flows to determine discrete affine scale ($S$) and zero-point ($Z$) quantization parameters, requiring no structural parameter retraining [21], [23].
2. **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** A retraining regimen that explicitly models integer rounding and clipping errors during backpropagation using simulated fake-quantization operators and the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) [22], [26], allowing stochastic gradient descent (SGD) to navigate parameter weights toward loss basins that are resilient to low-precision truncation [19].

In mission-critical security environments, deep traffic classifiers do not operate in isolation. Instead, they serve as automated front-line sensory feeds within modern Security Operations Center (SOC) pipelines and Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR) platforms [47], [48]. In these automated workflows, high-confidence classifier detections trigger downstream algorithmic actions: enriching SIEM alert telemetry, prioritizing incident escalation queues, and automatically synthesizing dynamic Access Control List (ACL) firewall drop rules (e.g., via `iptables`, `nftables`, or software-defined flow tables) targeted at specific packet sequence properties [49]–[51].

Crucially, integrating deep neural classifiers into automated defense workflows is increasingly governed by stringent international regulatory and compliance standards. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689) explicitly classifies AI systems deployed as safety components in the management and operation of critical digital infrastructure (Annex III) as *high-risk*, subjecting them to strict mandates regarding transparency (Article 13) and effective human oversight (Article 14) [54]. Article 13 mandates that high-risk AI systems must be accompanied by intelligible explanations that enable deployers to interpret system outputs and verify their underlying decision logic. Similarly, Article 22 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes the right of individuals not to be subjected to decisions based solely on automated processing that produce significant legal or operational impacts without access to meaningful information concerning the logic involved [53]. Furthermore, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) dictates that deployed artificial intelligence models must maintain demonstrable validity, reliability, safety, and explainability fidelity across all phases of the model compression and operational deployment lifecycle [55].

## B. The Unexamined Assumption: Predictive Invariance vs. Explainability Fidelity
To fulfill these operational and regulatory imperatives, the cybersecurity literature has widely embraced post-hoc Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks—most prominently cooperative game-theoretic Shapley attributions (KernelSHAP [30]) and local linear surrogates (LIME [31])—to audit black-box traffic classifiers [35]–[38]. These techniques compute localized feature attribution vectors $\boldsymbol{\phi} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ that quantify the marginal contribution of each input token (e.g., the length or arrival latency of a specific packet in the sequence) to the model's final classification logit. By inspecting these attributions, security analysts and audit algorithms verify whether a classifier is attending to authentic protocol semantics (such as initial TLS Client/Server Hello handshakes, cipher suite negotiations, or key exchange exchanges) rather than learning spurious background correlations (such as ephemeral packet lengths or transport-layer ACK timings) [49], [52].

However, the intersection of fixed-point model compression and explainability audits reveals a dangerous, systemic blind spot in modern edge engineering. In both academic literature and commercial deployment pipelines, the integrity of a quantized neural network is certified almost universally through a single, narrow heuristic: **predictive accuracy invariance** [19], [20]. If a quantized INT8 model preserves the top-line macro classification metrics of its uncompressed FP32 parent—exhibiting an accuracy, Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (ROC-AUC), or Macro $F_1$ degradation of less than one percentage point ($\Delta \text{Acc} < 1\%$)—the compression process is certified as successful and lossless. Under this paradigm, practitioners, network architects, and compliance auditors operate under the tacit, unexamined assumption that **predictive invariance implies explainability invariance**: that if a compressed model reaches the same classification output, it must necessarily do so by evaluating the same underlying decision logic.

We argue that this assumption is fundamentally flawed. A deep neural network represents an overparameterized, non-convex mathematical mapping possessing an astronomical degree of internal representational redundancy [42]–[44]. In complex multi-channel sequence spaces, there exist countless degenerate combinations of weight vectors and activation paths that produce identical output decisions on a given evaluation benchmark while relying on completely divergent input feature dependencies [39], [40]. Recent investigations in computer vision [45] and natural language processing [46] have cautioned that model compression can induce representational drift. Yet, within the domain of **1D temporal network traffic classifiers**, the stability of internal decision logic under discrete integer quantization remains entirely unquantified.

This disconnect introduces severe, uncharacterized operational risks into automated cybersecurity infrastructure:
1. **Silent Logic Corruption:** A quantized classifier deployed on an edge firewall may correctly classify a malicious VPN tunnel with $98\%$ recall, yet base that classification not on the structural handshake dynamics recognized by the floating-point reference model, but on arbitrary background noise or late-packet payload sizes. 
2. **Brittle SOAR and ACL Synthesis:** If automated SOAR platforms extract feature attributions from an uncompressed baseline model to synthesize firewall filtering rules, but the physical edge appliance executes a quantized model governed by restructured logic, the generated defense rules will target packet properties that the deployed appliance ignores, producing brittle, easily evadable perimeters [50]–[52].
3. **Regulatory Non-Compliance:** If post-deployment audits or regulatory inspections (under EU AI Act Articles 13/14 or GDPR Article 22) evaluate explanations derived from floating-point development models while the production gateway runs a quantized engine with divergent attribution pathways, the resulting transparency disclosures are demonstrably invalid.

Understanding whether, how, and why feature attributions drift under fixed-point quantization is therefore not merely a theoretical inquiry—it is an urgent operational requirement for trustworthy, edge-native network defense.

## C. Problem Statement and Formal Research Questions
This paper provides the first comprehensive, forensic investigation into the stability of feature attributions, probability calibration, and execution dynamics in low-bit quantized deep neural network traffic classifiers deployed on edge execution runtimes. We formally designate this phenomenon **Attribution Drift**: the geometric rotation, magnitude redistribution, and rank reorganization of local input feature explanations induced across the model compression and edge serialization lifecycle.

To systematically characterize this phenomenon, we formulate four foundational Research Questions (RQs):
- **RQ1 (Attribution Fidelity vs. Explainer Noise Floor):** *Does static Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) induce feature attribution drift that exceeds the intrinsic, stochastic Monte Carlo sampling variability of the post-hoc explainer itself, and are dominant protocol feature rankings preserved under discrete integer mapping?*
- **RQ2 (Structural Restructuring in Retrained Models):** *How does Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)—which explicitly minimizes classification loss under simulated low-precision constraints—alter the geometric orientation, magnitude concentration, and monotonic rank ordering of feature attributions relative to the floating-point baseline?*
- **RQ3 (Causal Disentanglement of Quantization vs. Optimization):** *Is attribution drift in retrained models caused by discrete integer rounding noise and low-precision parameter grids, or is it an artifact of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) trajectory displacement across an overparameterized, non-convex loss landscape?*
- **RQ4 (Numerical Serialization, Saturation Ceilings, and Execution Dynamics):** *How do fixed-point integer operator serialization and runtime execution constraints impact output probability calibration, operational decision boundaries, and real-time inference latency across streaming versus batched edge deployment regimes?*

## D. Summary of Empirical Discoveries
To resolve these questions with absolute empirical rigor, we design and execute **XDrift**, an end-to-end experimental framework evaluated on the University of New Brunswick Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC) **ISCX VPN-NonVPN 2016** benchmark [4]. To eliminate the catastrophic session-leakage vulnerabilities that afflict prior traffic classification studies [48], our ingestion pipeline reconstructs **339,009 bidirectional network flows** from 140 raw PCAP/PCAPNG capture files ($24.42\text{ GiB}$) and enforces a strict, leakage-free **canonical session-group partitioning** across 106 independent communication sessions. This yields a massive held-out evaluation test partition of **$N = 49,251$ bidirectional flows** originating from 16 canonical capture sessions, preserving the realistic operational class imbalance of production networks ($2,693$ VPN flows representing a $5.47\%$ minority class vs. $46,558$ Non-VPN flows). 

We train a lightweight, edge-oriented 1D-CNN (`Traffic1DCNN`, 44,129 parameters) ingesting $(2, 64)$ feature tensors (Channel 0: directional packet length normalized to $[-1, +1]$; Channel 1: log-scaled IAT in $[0, 1]$), optimized with positive class loss weighting ($\text{pos\_weight} = 30.3756$). We compress this model to true fixed-point INT8 representations via both static PTQ (HistogramObserver calibration on 69,564 validation flows) and QAT (5-epoch fine-tuning via STE) deployed under the PyTorch QNNPACK ARM64 runtime. To audit feature attributions, we implement **Masked KernelSHAP** ($M=50$ background reference flows, $K=150$ coalition evaluations per flow) across a representative, stratified test cohort of $N = 100$ flows ($50$ VPN and $50$ Non-VPN flows spanning sequence lengths from 4 to 64 packets), strictly masking out zero-padding positions to prevent synthetic ranking inflation.

Our experimental investigation yields six primary scientific discoveries:
1. **The Empirical Explainer Noise Floor:** We discover that perturbation-based explainers exhibit non-trivial Monte Carlo sampling stochasticity. By benchmarking KernelSHAP across three independent random seeds (Seeds 42, 43, 44) on the identical, unchanged FP32 model, we establish the empirical noise floor: Mean Top-5 Intersection-over-Union ($\text{IoU}_5$) $= 0.9553 \pm 0.1829$, Signed Cosine Similarity ($S_c$) $= 0.9676 \pm 0.1450$, Total Variation Distance ($\text{TV}$) $= 0.0553 \pm 0.1588$, and Spearman rank correlation ($r_s$) $= 0.9516 \pm 0.1666$. Any attribution shift failing to exceed these boundaries represents explainer jitter rather than true architectural drift.
2. **PTQ Preserves Dominant Decision Logic:** Contrary to the hypothesis of catastrophic explainability collapse, static PTQ preserves primary feature attributions with remarkable fidelity. In **95 of the 100 cohort flows**, PTQ selects the exact same Top-5 features as the uncompressed FP32 baseline ($\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9786 \pm 0.0970$), maintaining near-perfect directional alignment ($S_c = 0.9918 \pm 0.0394$) and minimal mass displacement ($\text{TV} = 0.0369 \pm 0.0620$). While a non-parametric paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test against the noise floor detects a statistically significant positive rank shift ($W = 1506.0, R^+ = 3544.0 > R^- = 1506.0, p = 0.000459$), the median shift magnitude is exceptionally small ($\text{median } \Delta \text{TV} = +0.004908$). For static quantization, explanations generated from floating-point reference models transfer reliably to edge deployments.
3. **QAT Induces Severe Structural Restructuring:** While QAT achieves superior predictive discrimination on the held-out test set ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9834$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.6478$, calibrated $F_1 = 0.7762$), its internal attribution geometry collapses. Signed cosine similarity plunges to $S_c = 0.4830 \pm 0.3449$ ($p < 10^{-17}$), Total Variation surges more than six-fold to $\text{TV} = 0.2346 \pm 0.1908$, and only $28\%$ of flows retain identical Top-5 features. On minority VPN flows, directional alignment deteriorates to $S_c = 0.1665 \pm 0.1406$ (median $0.1341$, Mann-Whitney $U = 89.0, p = 1.24 \times 10^{-15}$). QAT navigates to an alternative parameter space that achieves high predictive accuracy through fundamentally restructured decision rules.
4. **Causal Disentanglement via the FP32-FT Confounder Control:** By engineering an unquantized 5-epoch fine-tuning control baseline (`FP32-FT`) trained with the identical AdamW optimizer, learning rate, and loss schedule as QAT but in full 32-bit precision, we definitively resolve the root cause of attribution drift. FP32-FT induces massive attribution reorganization ($\text{TV} = 0.2214 \pm 0.2327$, median $0.2070$; $S_c = 0.1166 \pm 0.8106$; $\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9481$). A paired Wilcoxon equivalence test confirms that attribution drift between QAT and FP32-FT is statistically indistinguishable ($W = 2390.0, p = 0.684, \text{median } \Delta \text{TV} = -0.0047$). This proves definitively that **attribution restructuring is driven by gradient descent optimization navigating an overparameterized loss surface**, not by integer quantization noise.
5. **Output Layer Saturation and the Threshold Collapse Pitfall:** We discover that under QNNPACK integer serialization, the output linear layer's unsigned 8-bit quantization parameters ($S = 0.02535125, Z = 215, q_{\max} = 255$) impose a rigid mathematical ceiling on the pre-sigmoid logit ($z_{\max} = 1.01405$), capping predicted probabilities at $p_{\max} = \sigma(1.01405) = \mathbf{0.733812}$. This ceiling clamps **$98.11\%$ of all test VPN flows ($2,642$ of $2,693$)** to the identical maximal score. Consequently, directly transferring the baseline operating threshold ($\tau = 0.900$) to the deployed QAT model causes **total operational failure: zero positive calls, $0.0\%$ recall, and an $F_1$-score of $0.0000$**. Threshold recalibration on validation data ($\tau = 0.680$) fully restores detection capability ($F_1 = 0.7762$, recall $= 98.33\%$).
6. **Microarchitectural Edge Latency Duality:** Hardware profiling on Apple Silicon ARM64 reveals a fundamental operational dichotomy. Under single-flow streaming inspection ($B = 1$), INT8 quantization achieves only a modest $1.05\times$ speedup ($0.267\text{ ms} \to 0.255\text{ ms}$) because runtime interpretation, memory allocation, and operator dispatch overhead consume $>99\%$ of CPU cycles. Conversely, under micro-batched triage ($B = 64$), SIMD vectorization across 128-bit ARM NEON registers unlocks an order-of-magnitude **$12\times$ throughput acceleration** ($6,387 \to 76,265\text{ flows/s}$) and compresses the checkpoint by $3.56\times$ ($184.2\text{ KB} \to 51.8\text{ KB}$), fitting the static model entirely within on-chip L1 data cache.

## E. Concrete Research Contributions
This paper provides four concrete, foundational contributions to the fields of network security, edge computing, and explainable artificial intelligence:
1. **Methodological & Forensic Benchmarking Protocol:** We establish an empirically grounded protocol for auditing XAI stability under model compression. This includes: (i) an uncompressed multi-seed explainer noise floor protocol to distinguish quantization drift from Monte Carlo sampling jitter; (ii) temporal sequence masking to prevent synthetic ranking inflation over zero-padded tokens; and (iii) a leakage-free canonical session-grouping methodology (106 groups, 339,009 flows) that guarantees zero cross-split session contamination on the ISCX VPN-NonVPN benchmark.
2. **Empirical Disentanglement of Quantization vs. Optimization:** We formulate and validate an unquantized fine-tuning ablation baseline (`FP32-FT`), proving through non-parametric paired Wilcoxon equivalence testing ($W = 2390.0, p = 0.684$) that QAT attribution restructuring is caused by gradient descent optimization navigating an overparameterized loss surface toward an alternative parameter basin, whereas discrete integer quantization alone (PTQ) preserves attribution geometry ($\text{IoU}_5 = 0.9786, S_c = 0.9918$).
3. **Mechanistic Derivation of Output Saturation & Threshold Dynamics:** We analytically derive and empirically confirm the discrete UINT8 serialization ceiling ($z_{\max} = 1.01405 \implies p_{\max} = 0.733812$) that clamps $98.11\%$ of minority VPN flows. We demonstrate that naive threshold transfer causes complete detection collapse ($F_1 = 0.0000$), establish post-quantization validation recalibration as an indispensable deployment requirement ($F_1 = 0.7762$), and expose why aggregate Brier and ECE metrics produce misleading confidence assessments under majority-class probability compression.
4. **Microarchitectural Profiling of Edge Inference Engines:** We deliver a rigorous microarchitectural characterization of fixed-point network traffic inspection on ARM64 hardware running PyTorch QNNPACK, quantifying the performance boundary between framework-bound streaming execution ($1.05\times$ latency reduction) and compute-bound batched SIMD acceleration ($11.94\times$ to $12.77\times$ throughput scaling, $76\text{k}$--$82\text{k flows/s}$), and establishing that the $3.56\times$ footprint reduction (51.8 KB) enables persistent L1-D cache residency on edge processors.

## F. Paper Organization Roadmap
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: 
- **Section II (Related Work):** Reviews the evolution of encrypted traffic representation learning, fixed-point neural network quantization architectures, post-hoc explainability methods in network security, and establishes a comparative taxonomy positioning XDrift against existing literature.
- **Section III (Methodology & Forensic Protocols):** Details the canonical session-grouping pipeline, the 1D-CNN architecture, the mathematical formulation of PTQ and QAT quantization operators under PyTorch QNNPACK, the implementation of the unquantized FP32-FT confounder control, the Masked KernelSHAP attribution engine, and the paired non-parametric statistical framework.
- **Section IV (Experimental Results & Hardware Benchmarks):** Presents the empirical evaluation on the 49,251-flow held-out test partition and 100-flow attribution cohort, covering physical ARM64 hardware latency and throughput, predictive discrimination, mathematical derivation and empirical verification of output probability saturation, attribution drift metrics, causal disentanglement testing, and 16-group clustered bootstrap inference.
- **Section V (Discussion & Threats to Validity):** Discusses the operational security consequences of attribution drift in SOAR pipelines, analyzes compliance implications under the EU AI Act and GDPR, examines the limitations of empirical session skew, and outlines defensive engineering guidelines for edge AI deployment.
- **Section VI (Conclusion):** Concludes the paper and outlines future directions for attribution-regularized edge quantization objectives.
